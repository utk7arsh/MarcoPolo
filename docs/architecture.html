The app installs and launches on the iPhone, but the full "where are my keys?" run, from the vest to the server and back into the AirPods, has not happened yet. What the wearer can say (and do)

Every command the product is meant to support, how it flows, and how far along it is. Circled numbers are wires in the diagram below.

Find
"Marco, where are my keys?"

Answers from memory, then guides you with sound until your hand touches them (the 7 stages below). Not seen yet? Marco asks you to turn slowly and keeps watching for 60 s: "Polo! I see your keys…"

Mic → on-device speech → query find ④ → world model → answer ⑧ → 60 Hz guidance ⑪
Identify
"Marco, what is this?"

Known item: plays its label, in the labeller's voice if they recorded one. Unknown: asks the caretaker (next card).

Touch from the hand stream ④ → what_is_this → label audio ⑧
Caretaker labels

label_request ⑨ → iMessage ⑫ → reply ② → POST /v1/labels ⑥ → label_ready ⑧

Left behind
"Marco, this is the door."

set_place door ④ → event left_behind ⑧
Operator

The find experience, stage by stage

What the wearer hears after "where are my keys?". All seven stages exist in code (ios/Marco/Core/Guidance.swift, Lines.swift, server touch.py); none has been heard on the vest yet. The demo needs every one of them.

"Marco, where are my keys?"
Wake word + on-device speech; the name is matched to a registered item.
Voice/Listener, Lines.Speech
"Polo! Behind you, two steps," from the keys
Direction relative to the body, sound placed at the item.
Lines.polo, SpatialAudio
Low beep from the side to turn to
Never played behind you: a dull beep from your left or right while it's behind, a plain one in front.
Guidance phase behind
Turn: double ding + a buzz when facing
A 30 cm corridor, not a cone; drift off it and the beep changes at once.
Guidance phase facing
Walk: bright pulses, faster, then three notes: stop
Pace is the distance across the room; the stop notes come early enough for your speed.
Guidance phase approach
Reach: pings climb, then a steady tone
A parking sensor for the hand, from the fingertip's distance (server, 15/s).
server touch.py → Guidance phase reach
Touch: silence, then "Got it."
Or the item's own label, in the labeller's voice.
server touch → Guidance phase touched

The system: pre-processing → Vultr → post-processing

Read left to right. Everything that comes in (sensors, speech, caretaker replies) is captured and packaged on the left; the brain on Vultr turns it into memory and decisions; everything that goes out (sound, labels, alerts, the dashboard) is rendered on the right. The phone, the AirPods, the Photon bridge and the caretaker appear on both sides because they both send and receive.
