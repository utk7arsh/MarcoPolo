## Q6 — Distance perception

### S1. Kolarik, Moore, Zahorik, Cirstea & Pardhan (2016). Auditory distance perception in humans: a review of cues, development, neuronal bases, and effects of sensory loss. Atten Percept Psychophys 78(2):373–395. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4744263/ (local copy papers/psychoacoustics/kolarik2016_review/)

- Bias pattern (near over-, far under-estimation; best ~1 m) [PRIMARY]: "On average, perceived distance to sound sources in peripersonal space tends to be overestimated, while distance to sounds in extrapersonal space is generally underestimated ... Auditory distance judgments are generally most accurate for sound sources approximately 1 m from the listener." Their Fig. 1 = headphone simulation, noise bursts, virtual distances 0.3–14 m (from Zahorik & Wightman 2001).
- Model form [PRIMARY]: "compressive power functions of the form r′ = k r^a gave good fits to distance judgments ... (with a < 1)" (attributed to Zahorik et al. 2005; numeric k,a not given in this review — see S2).
- Level cue [PRIMARY]: inverse-square "level falls by approximately 6 dB for each doubling of the source distance"; in Zahorik's (2002a) small auditorium "the rate was approximately 4 dB/doubling"; reverberant level drops "only about 1 dB for each doubling". Level is a RELATIVE cue (source level unknown) -> "absolute distance judgments for single sound sources based solely on level difficult or impossible".
- Distance-discrimination thresholds (level as main cue) [PRIMARY, review summary]: ~6% (Ashmead et al. 1990, white noise), 13% (Simpson & Stanton 1973), ~20% (Edwards 1955; Gamble 1909), 25% (Akeroyd et al. 2007); predicted from level JND (0.4 dB noise; 1–2 dB tones) = 5–25% of reference distance.
- DRR JNDs [PRIMARY]: wideband noise 2–3 dB at 0 and +10 dB DRR, ~6–8 dB at −10 and +20 dB DRR (Larsen et al. 2008); Zahorik (2002b) 5–6 dB for DRR 0–20 dB.
- Near-field [PRIMARY]: "ILD provides a distance cue for distances up to approximately 1 m, beyond which it becomes roughly independent of source distance"; "spectral cues do not provide distance information for sounds located in the range 1-15 m"; HRTFs "almost independent of sound source distance beyond 1 m". Pure tones: listeners "were unable to judge the distances of pure-tone stimuli" (Wightman & Firestone 1930; Molino 1973) — possibly because tones are not externalized (Duda & Martens 1998).
- Head movement adds little for distance [PRIMARY]: "Only small benefits of head movements ... (Gardner 1969)"; "Simpson and Stanton (1973) found that head movements did not aid judgments of distance for pulse trains at distances between 0.3 and 2.66 m."
- Dynamic cues while walking [PRIMARY]: Speigle & Loomis (1993) 20-Hz pulse trains 2–6 m outdoors: "dynamic cues led to small improvements"; Ashmead et al. (1995) 5–19 m: walking-to-target accuracy better when listening while walking; but Teramoto et al. (2012) "For tone bursts presented up to 1.5 m away, self-motion resulted in greater errors".
- Walking to sound (blind-walking) [PRIMARY]: Loomis et al. (1998) speech 4–16 m: far sounds underestimated, verbal and walking responses consistent, verbal more variable. Russell & Schneider (2006) 0.7 kHz tones 1.6–3.1 m: walking more accurate than verbal.
- Blindness [PRIMARY]: relative distance discrimination BETTER in blind (Voss et al. 2004: 3–4 m; Kolarik et al. 2013b: 1–8 m), absolute distance NOT better and often worse (early-blind overestimate near and underestimate far; Kolarik 2013c speech 1.2–13.8 m; Macé et al. 2012 reach-space pointing errors larger in blind). Distance discrimination thresholds for blind ~0.8 m at 2 m reference and ~1.5 m at 5 m reference (Kolarik 2013b; sighted larger).
- Blind cane users have extended peripersonal space at the cane tip (Serino et al. 2007) [PRIMARY summary].
- Implication [DERIVED]: for 0.5–6 m indoors, rendered level+DRR can give ordinal (closer/farther) information and change-over-time cues, but absolute distance from audio alone will be compressed (and blind users show bias); explicit distance coding (speech "2 meters", tempo) is warranted.
