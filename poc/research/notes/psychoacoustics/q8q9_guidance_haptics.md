## 1. Loomis, Marston, Golledge & Klatzky (2005) — PGS display comparison (blind, outdoor GPS)
Citation: Loomis JM, Marston JR, Golledge RG, Klatzky RL (2005). Personal guidance system for people with visual impairment: A comparison of spatial displays for route guidance. J Vis Impair Blind 99(4):219-232. PMC2801896.
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2801896/ (full text read via curl -> text; local copy papers/psychoacoustics/loomis2005_jvib/pmc.txt, fig3.jpg, fig4.jpg)

- Participants [PRIMARY]: "Fifteen people who were legally blind (9 men and 6 women) ... average age was 39 (SD = 14.7) ... Six participants had no useful vision" ; residual-vision participants blindfolded; all used long cane during test.
- Task [PRIMARY]: "Each path was 50 meters (164 feet) long and had six turns, with seven segments of variable length" (three 45°, two 90°, one 135° turns; mean segment 7.1 m). Waypoint captured "within 2.1 meters (about 7 feet)". GPS absolute error "usually less than 1 meter". Head/hand/torso compass.
- Display designs [PRIMARY] (very relevant to "repeat a call ~1/s"):
  - Virtual speech: spatialized synthetic speech of the distance in feet, "an utterance giving distance (such as "24") was given 72 times per minute" (= every 0.83 s), from the waypoint direction over headphones, intensity rising with approach.
  - Virtual tone: spatialized tones; on-course (head within ±10° of waypoint) tone = triangular 560 Hz, "five times per second, with a duration of 160 milliseconds and a silent interval of 40 milliseconds"; off-course (>10°) = frequency-swept "whooping" tone "at the rate of 2.3 times per second"; spatialized distance speech every 8 s. "One tone or the other was always present".
  - HPI tone: hand-held compass pointer; beeps (same 5/s on-course tone) from shoulder speaker when hand within 10° of waypoint; speech bearing ("120 left") if >90° off.
  - HPI speech: "straight" within 10°, "left"/"right" for 10–90°, bearing in degrees beyond 90°.
  - Body pointing: same as HPI tone but compass at the waist/torso (within 10°).
- Overall [PRIMARY]: all 15 completed all 10 trials. "mean actual distance traveled ... was just 62 meters" for 50 m paths (path ratio ~1.24), "mean travel time was 110 seconds ... average walking speed of .56 meters ... per second" (6 turns, cane, GPS).
- Travel time by display, all 15 participants [approx, read from Fig. 3]: HPI tone ~126 s, HPI speech ~114 s, body pointing ~112 s, virtual tone ~100 s, virtual speech ~94 s (SE bars ~6-13 s). Travel distance [approx, Fig. 3]: ~59-64 m for all displays (no significant difference).
- Six fastest participants [PRIMARY]: "HPI tone = 100 seconds, HPI speech = 103.8 seconds, body pointing = 92.3 seconds, virtual tone = 86.4 seconds, and virtual speech = 70.7 seconds."
- Stats [PRIMARY]: "significant effect of display on travel time, F(4,56) = 4.92, p < .01, but no effect on travel distance ... Virtual speech led to faster travel than all other displays except virtual tone, and virtual tone led to faster travel than HPI tone."
- Subjective [approx, read from Fig. 4]: rating (1-10) virtual speech ~8.0, body pointing ~7.3, HPI tone ~6.4, HPI speech ~5.7, virtual tone ~5.3. [PRIMARY] "Virtual speech was judged better than all the other displays except body pointing".
- Comments [PRIMARY]: "Several complained about the continuous presence of tones in the virtual-tone display, and one person said that the speech was more localizable than the tones." "a few participants liked the pointer because it was faster for locating a waypoint than turning the body with the body-pointing display ... several noted the sluggishness or difficulty of scanning with the body." Many negative comments about headphones blocking environmental sounds.
- Caveat [PRIMARY]: virtual speech gave continuously updated distance; others only every 8 s.
- Relevance: a spatialized, repeated (~1.2/s) speech call over head-tracked headphones was the fastest and best-rated display for blind users; continuous tones were disliked. Speech (broadband) was reported more localizable than the 560 Hz tone by one participant (anecdotal).
